# Payment workshop

The header-free presentation lives at `/workshop`. `/workshop/partner` and `/workshop/merchant` are standalone editions. The audience is the buyer: ISOs/payment providers reselling white-label infrastructure, and merchants seeking persistent acceptance and payment-gap coverage. The shared story runs in order: business case, processor disruption, two-leg fiat funding, cross-chain crypto, KYC/AML controls, continuity, managed infrastructure, relevant audience chapters, buyer launch benefits and launch plan. Facilitation instructions belong only in optional presenter notes. The active deployment supplies its name through BrandContext. Product screenshots are explicitly labeled as sample workspaces.

Use the bottom controls to advance, choose a chapter, switch editions, enter fullscreen or show presenter prompts. Arrow Left / Right and Page Up / Down navigate chapters when focus is outside an interactive control. N toggles presenter prompts. Ordinary scrolling remains available. Presenter prompts are visible on the presentation screen and are off initially.

The selected business gap carries through to the closing conversation and copied workshop brief. Nothing is submitted or persisted. The brief includes both transaction legs, the pilot surface, industry/region review, commercial terms, support ownership and a first reconciled sale. Clipboard failures expose a selectable text version. Screenshot buttons open an accessible, scrollable lightbox. Motion follows the reduced-motion preference. Both transaction legs stay visible together; the four selectable stages expand the explanation rather than hiding the core process.

Content and presenter prompts live in `src/components/workshop/content.ts`. Visual chapters are in `WorkshopPresentation.tsx`; the route adapter is `WorkshopPage.tsx`. Add a chapter to the content list and its corresponding section to the component. Keep IDs aligned for navigation and deep links. The three route pages only select an audience.

`WorkshopExplorers.tsx` contains the illustrative per-merchant split configurator and the cross-chain payment diagram. Neither tool requests a quote, executes a payment or modifies real merchant configuration.

The renderer is imported and initialized near the viewport, limits pixel density, renders at approximately 30fps and stops drawing offscreen. Scroll progress drives interpolated camera and shopper movement without per-frame React updates. Resize observation keeps projection and product markers aligned. The motion control and OS reduced-motion preference use static scene poses and disable ambient movement. A text-based scene fallback preserves the story and controls when WebGL is unavailable. Geometry, materials, textures, listeners and observers are disposed on unmount.

The partner network uses CSS perspective; the merchant scene uses the `three` runtime and locally generated geometry and textures, with no external model service. Existing public product screenshots are loaded lazily. The rendered QR contains demonstration text only; scanning it does not initiate a payment. Inventory records, fund movement and payroll/supplier uses are illustrative, with availability and payout conditions stated in the scene. All interaction is local; the presentation makes no payment, account or partner-configuration mutations.

The store uses procedural stone and walnut textures, brushed brass fittings, a fluted counter, arched branding and warm interior lighting. `store-people.ts` builds human-scale, faceless characters with smooth head silhouettes, simple hair, tailored clothing, articulated limbs and hands, and shaped shoes with thin rounded soles that move with the ankles. ## Payment claims

The presentation explains the existing Stripe onramp, not a new Stripe integration. Stripe's role in the crypto purchase and reuse of saved details are described in its [onramp documentation](https://docs.stripe.com/crypto/onramp). The platform's USDC/Base configuration and subsequent settlement are evidenced in `src/app/api/stripe/onramp-quote/route.ts` and `src/app/api/stripe/background-poll/route.ts`. Payment-method selection is implemented in `src/lib/stripe-onramp-payment-methods.ts`. The workshop distinguishes funding from platform settlement and does not promise universal eligibility, instant settlement, fixed savings or guaranteed revenue. Revalidate these claims before changing the payment story.

The recovery example reflects `background-poll/route.ts`: settlement execution claims, session-specific settlement amounts, recorded transaction hashes, prior-transfer checks and guarded retries. Wallet ownership verification handling is in `src/lib/stripe-wallet-ownership.ts`. These are transaction safeguards, not proof of an uptime percentage or an independent AML certification. Stripe performs the described onramp KYC and sanctions screening; merchant eligibility remains subject to the applicable product, industry and regional requirements, including [Stripe's restrictions](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses).

The requested 99.95% figure needs a measurement period and scope, or a confirmed target/SLA designation, before being presented as a quantified commitment. No supporting uptime report or SLA was found in the reviewed repository material. Likewise, industry-leading compliance and nearly universal high-risk acceptance need supporting evidence and an approved coverage list. The current presentation explains concrete controls and higher-risk merchant needs without making those unverified claims. Once substantiated, place the availability metric in the continuity chapter with its scope, period and source.

The 17,000+ tokens / 90+ blockchains scope is the product coverage supplied by the product owner and is consistent with the existing homepage's 17,000-token / 95-chain positioning (`src/components/landing/home-content.tsx`). It is not a live inventory fetched by this page. Supported routes remain dependent on liquidity, token pairs and network conditions, as described in the [bridge FAQ](https://portal.thirdweb.com/bridge/faq). `portal-preview-embedded.tsx` configures the crypto-only CheckoutWidget with the selected destination token and Base chain; SOL is an ERC-20 representation on Base. Recheck the product coverage as provider support changes.

Per-merchant overrides and agent shares are supported in `src/lib/thirdweb/split.ts`; payment-method routing is in `src/lib/payment-split-routing.ts`. The reserve documentation distinguishes allocation when funds arrive in a split from release to recipient wallets. The presentation therefore says instant allocation on settlement, and explicitly describes the subsequent distribution/withdrawal flow, rather than promising instant end-to-end wallet or bank payouts. The example rates are not a commercial quote or a claim that all deployed contracts can be edited in place.
